# E-Commerce Website UI/UX Revamp & Enhancement Scope
### Transcom Digital — Electronics & Home Appliance Platform | Bangladesh

> **One clear message:** The core objective of this project is to comprehensively modernize and revamp the existing e-commerce website UI/UX, creating a cleaner, smoother, easier-to-use shopping experience from product discovery through checkout. Personalized product recommendations and AI-driven shopping enhancements are valuable opportunities, but they are optional add-ons and should remain separate from the primary UI/UX revamp scope.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This scope is based on a full review of the live Transcom Digital website — covering how customers discover, compare, afford, and purchase high-consideration electronics (AC, TV, refrigerator, washing machine, kitchen and small appliances) — validated against e-commerce experience patterns observed across leading global and regional platforms.

**The experience today is content-present but interaction-immature.** The catalog is broad and correctly structured, but core shopping interactions create friction where confidence matters most: key browse paths do not reliably reach products, product listing controls do not behave as expected, delivery cost is hidden until late, and purchase confidence (what's included, what fits, what it will really cost monthly) is not visible where the buyer decides. Journeys that require research and comparison — typical for a Tk 50,000–150,000 appliance — lose momentum across sessions.

**Primary focus:** Modernize and simplify the *existing* shopping experience first. Deliver a cleaner visual design, simpler navigation, easier discovery, clearer product information, and a smooth three-step checkout — so every customer can move from *exploring* to *confident decision* with fewer steps and less uncertainty. This core revamp is the primary scope and must deliver value independently.

**Optional enhancement:** Once the core is stable, personalized and AI-driven recommendations can be layered on top to make discovery more relevant over time. They are valuable, but they depend on data readiness and should be proposed, estimated, and decided on separately — they do not redefine the primary objective.

**Strategic direction:** Fix what blocks the journey, make the right product easier to find and understand, and make the total cost to own transparent before checkout. Add memory (recently viewed, continue shopping) before adding personalization intelligence.

---

## 2. Project Objectives

The project aims to:

1.  **Revamp the UI/UX as the primary scope** — deliver a smoother, cleaner, more modern and user-friendly shopping experience across the entire journey.
2.  **Make shopping easier** — simplify navigation, information hierarchy, and category discovery so customers understand where they are and how to move next.
3.  **Make product discovery faster** — improve homepage orientation, category exploration, search, filtering, sorting, and product cards so customers reach relevant products in fewer steps.
4.  **Make product decisions more confident** — present specifications, benefits, imagery, offers, delivery and trust information in a clear, consistent hierarchy that helps customers compare without being overwhelmed.
5.  **Make bundles and offers discoverable in context** — surface relevant combinations, campaigns and complementary products without creating promotional clutter.
6.  **Make checkout smoother and more efficient** — introduce a simple three-step flow with clarity, progress visibility and mobile-friendly forms.
7.  **Improve usability and consistency** — unify visual, interaction and component behavior across all key flows, including empty, loading and error states.
8.  **Remove existing UX pain points** that currently create hesitation, repetition and drop-off.
9.  **Deliver a fully responsive experience** that is appropriate for desktop, tablet and mobile — not merely a shrunken desktop layout.
10. **Position optional enhancements separately** — keep personalized recommendations as an add-on scope that can be considered once the core delivers value.

**Priority hierarchy is explicit:** Core UI/UX modernization first; personalized recommendations as an optional second layer.

## 4. Core UI/UX Revamp Scope

*This is the largest and most important section. It represents the primary scope and priority.*

### 4.1 Modern & Clean Visual Design

**Current challenge:** The current interface is functional but visually dense — multiple competing grids on the homepage, dense product cards with overlapping badges, and inconsistent spacing and typography across categories make scanning harder than it should be.

**Revamp direction:** A modern, clean design system with clear hierarchy, generous whitespace, consistent typography and a restrained badge system. Product cards will use one savings indicator (not two), one clear monthly installment cue where eligible, and a legible discount treatment. Promotional content will be curated, not stacked.

**Scope of improvement:** System-wide visual refresh applied consistently to homepage, listing, product, cart and checkout shells.

**Expected outcome:** A calmer, more scannable experience that elevates product imagery and price clarity while reducing visual fatigue.

### 4.2 Improved Overall User Experience

**Current challenge:** Core tasks — browse a category, narrow results, compare alternatives, understand total cost, complete purchase — require more steps and recall than necessary.

**Revamp direction:** Reduce steps and memory load at every stage. Every interaction will show clear system status (what is selected, what changed, what happens next) and offer an obvious next action.

**Scope of improvement:** End-to-end journey from homepage through checkout, including feedback, empty and error states.

**Expected outcome:** Less hesitation, fewer repeated searches, and a faster path from interest to decision.

### 4.3 Simplified Navigation & Information Architecture

**Current challenge:** Primary browse paths do not reliably reach products — key "See All" links and some category paths lead to dead ends — and the hierarchy between brand pages, category pages and search results is not immediately clear. Long category guides sit above product grids, pushing products below the fold.

**Revamp direction:** A rebuilt, reliable navigation with a clear mega-menu category structure that reflects the actual catalog (including correct handling of top-level categories and brand entry points). Category guides will be preserved for value but re-layered below product grids with a clear "Not sure? 30-second guide" entry point. Brand and search paths will be made distinct and canonical so customers know which is authoritative.

**Scope of improvement:** Primary header navigation, category structure, mega-menu, breadcrumbs, search entry, and key promotional entry points.

**Expected outcome:** Customers always reach real products, understand where they are, and can move to the next relevant category without hitting a wall.

### 4.4 Improved Product Discovery

**Current challenge:** Discovery is diffuse — the homepage presents many equally-weighted grids, and there is no trail to resume an interrupted research session. Customers must rebuild their path from the start when they return.

**Revamp direction:** A curated homepage with a focused hero, a few well-chosen category and deal blocks, and clear category entry. Add lightweight memory — recently viewed products and *continue where you left off* (last filtered listing or search restored) — that works for guests and persists after sign-in, without requiring advanced personalization to deliver value.

**Scope of improvement:** Homepage discovery, category exploration, related-product discovery, and resumption across sessions.

**Expected outcome:** Faster orientation for first-time visitors and one-tap resumption for returning researchers.

### 4.5 Bundle, Campaign & Offer Discovery

**Current challenge:** Bundle and offer value is real (e.g., "Complete the Setup" combinations like TV + mount + soundbar, AC + stabilizer) but not surfaced where the buying decision happens. Campaigns exist but are not consistently echoed on product and cart pages.

**Revamp direction:** Contextual, curated bundles shown where relevant — as a toggleable bundle row on the product page and a "Complete your setup" grouping in the cart — limited to categories where attachment is genuinely useful (TV, AC, refrigerator, washing machine) and suppressed for low-ticket items. Campaigns and offer validity will be shown inline on listing and product pages with clear savings language, not as disruptive banners.

**Scope of improvement:** Product and cart bundle presentation, campaign and offer inline visibility, complementary product discovery.

**Expected outcome:** One-trip shopping for relevant combinations with visible saving, without promotional clutter.

### 4.6 Product Browsing, Search & Filtering Experience

**Current challenge:** The sort control does not behave as expected, active filters are not summarized, result counts are unclear, pagination is ambiguous, and price controls compete (slider and overlapping buckets). Search has no visible guidance while typing, and typos or narrow filter combinations can lead to a dead end. Customer review filtering appears but has no usable values.

**Revamp direction:** Working, predictable browse controls: active-filter chips with `X filters applied` and one-tap clear, a real sort select (`Relevance, Price low→high, Price high→low, Newest, Discount`), and clear pagination (`Showing 1–12 of 45 — Show 12/24/48`). Price will use a single primary control (slider with preset ticks). Search will offer as-you-type suggestions by product, brand, category and model number (SKU-exact matches ranked first) plus recent searches, and forgiving recovery on no results (`Did you mean…`, `Remove Brand → 12 results`, `Try the AC finder`).

**Scope of improvement:** Product listing experience, product cards, search box and suggestions, filtering, sorting, pagination, zero-result handling.

**Expected outcome:** Customers can narrow to the right set quickly, see what is applied, and recover from typos without restarting.

### 4.7 Product Detail & Purchase Decision Experience

**Current challenge:** For high-consideration appliances, key purchase information is either hidden a click deep (key specs only after opening a tab, warranty phrasing shifts by category) or not visible at all — imagery is limited to static generics without zoom, no short product videos, delivery cost and installation eligibility are hidden behind a permission prompt, and reviews exist as tabs but show no aggregate rating.

**Revamp direction:** A clear, consistent product page hierarchy: title and variant (where relevant, e.g., tonnage `1 / 1.5`) → price with transparent monthly installment line (always visible where eligible) → total-cost row (`Upfront + Delivery + Install + Energy → EMI/month` with verdict `EMI not available → see EMI Bank List` where not eligible) → delivery and installation estimate by area/district *without* requiring location permission → key benefits in plain language → imagery that rewards inspection (hover zoom/pinch, gallery count, short 15–25s functional video per category template) → consistent tabs `Overview | Specifications | Reviews | Product Policy` → related alternatives. Plain-language explanations for technical terms (tap `EER 3.15` to see `≈ Tk Y/month`) and a humanized warranty legend (`Service/Parts/Compressor → 5 years`) will replace inconsistent shorthand.

**Scope of improvement:** Product information hierarchy, specifications, benefits, imagery, key purchase information, offers, delivery information, trust indicators, related and alternative products, and decision support.

**Expected outcome:** Complex products become easier to understand and compare at the exact moment of price exposure, with fewer visits to external search for meaning.

### 4.8 Cart Experience Improvements

**Current challenge:** The cart is fully functional but lacks reassurance — no confirmation when an item is added, the empty cart is a dead end with no path forward, and there is no grouped view of bundles.

**Revamp direction:** Clear feedback on every add (`Added to cart` confirmation with mini-cart), an empty-cart that suggests recently viewed and relevant entry points, and a cart that correctly groups bundle savings where applied.

**Scope of improvement:** Cart page and mini-cart, add-to-cart feedback, empty-cart state.

**Expected outcome:** Confidence that actions worked and an obvious next step when the cart is empty.

### 4.9 Simplified Three-Step Checkout Experience

**Objective: create a smoother, more efficient checkout process through a simple three-step flow.**

**Current challenge:** Checkout steps are not visible until late, progress is not indicated, the cost breakdown is unclear until the very end, and forms do not clearly signal what is required or what happens next — particularly on mobile.

**Recommended structure:**

*   **Step 1 — Customer & Delivery Information** — Contact, delivery address and area/district selection with simple, forgiving inputs and clear required-field guidance. Delivery timeline and fee are shown immediately once the area is entered (powered by the same estimator on the product page).
*   **Step 2 — Delivery & Payment** — Delivery method and time window plus equally prominent payment choices (Cash on Delivery, mobile wallets such as bKash/Nagad, cards, and EMI with tenure picker where eligible and district-aware `COD available` truth). Offline form latency for EMI is disclosed honestly.
*   **Step 3 — Order Review & Confirmation** — A complete, readable summary (`Subtotal + Delivery + Install → Total`, bundle saving, monthly EMI where applicable) with the ability to correct any step without losing progress.

**Design focus:** Clarity, speed, form simplicity (single phone field with formatting help, not split fields), visible progress (`Cart Review → Delivery & Payment → Confirmation`), inline validation and error handling, mobile-friendly inputs, and trust reinforcement (authorized-retailer, delivery and return clarity) alongside — not after — the cost.

**Expected outcome:** A transparent, predictable checkout that reduces abandonment — customers know the full landed cost and next step before they commit.

### 4.10 Responsive Experience Across Desktop, Tablet & Mobile

Responsive design is a **core requirement**, not a stretch goal.

*   **Desktop:** Full-width category navigation and mega-menu, multi-column product grids, hover interactions (zoom, quick view), spacious product galleries.
*   **Tablet:** Adapted grids (fewer columns, larger cards), off-canvas filters and mega-menu, thumb-reachable primary actions, preserved comparison usability.
*   **Mobile:** Thumb-first navigation (bottom or prominent drawer for categories), full-screen search with recent searches, off-canvas filters/sort with chip summary sticky on top, swipeable product galleries with count, sticky `Add to Cart` and installment line. Cart and three-step checkout will be single-column with large, forgiving inputs and progress always visible. Bundle rows will stack rather than compress.

The goal is a consistent but contextually appropriate experience — the same capability, interaction-optimized per device.

### 4.11 Usability & Experience Consistency

**Current challenge:** Layout, labels and component behavior shift between categories — warranty phrasing, tab sets, badge treatments and button hierarchy are not uniform, and feedback, empty, loading and error states are minimal.

**Revamp direction:** One design system applied consistently: predictable button hierarchy, uniform form patterns, consistent component behavior, and designed states for every journey — feedback (added/saved), empty (cart, wishlist, search), loading (search suggestions, filter apply) and error (validation, dead-end link guard). Icon-only controls will always have a text label.

**Scope of improvement:** Visual and interaction consistency across homepage, listing, product, cart and checkout, including all transient states and mobile interaction patterns.

**Expected outcome:** A predictable experience where customers learn once and apply everywhere, regardless of category.

### 4.12 Identification & Resolution of UX Pain Points

Major pain points consolidated from research — not hundreds of minor issues, but thematic friction that blocks journeys:

| Pain Point Area | Why It Creates Friction | Recommended Revamp Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Browse dead-ends** | Customers cannot reach products from key discovery links | Rebuilt navigation with guarded link generation and link health monitoring |
| **Listing cannot be narrowed confidently** | Placeholder sort, no active-filter summary, unclear page depth | Working sort, visible chips with clear result count (`Showing 1–12 of 45`) |
| **Homepage overload & no resumption** | Too many equally-weighted grids; returning researchers restart from zero | Curated homepage + lightweight `Recently Viewed / Continue` memory for guests |
| **Product decision unsupported** | Specs hidden, jargon unexplained, no comparison, no total-cost | Plain-language specs, tap-to-explain jargon, side-by-side compare, total-cost row at price |
| **Delivery & cost surprise** | Fee and install hidden behind permission; no landed cost | Area/district text input on product page → instant estimate; total-cost row with verdict |
| **Checkout opacity** | No step visibility or progress until very late | Explicit three-step flow with visible stepper and forgiving forms |
| **Trust dissipates at decision** | `Genuine Product` claim only on homepage, not where buyer pays | Authorized-retailer proof and warranty sheet shown at product and cart price context |

Each direction removes friction before adding new capability on top.

## 5. Improved End-to-End Shopping Journey

The revamp is designed as a continuous flow, not a set of isolated screens. Every stage connects to the next with visible status and an obvious path forward.

**DISCOVER → BROWSE → SEARCH / FILTER → EXPLORE PRODUCTS → COMPARE / DECIDE → ADD TO CART → CHECKOUT → ORDER CONFIRMATION**

*   **Discover** — A curated, scannable homepage makes category and brand entry clear, with memory (recently viewed, continue where you left off) so returning customers do not restart from zero.
*   **Browse** — Reliable navigation and mega-menu lead consistently to real product listings; key entry points for promotions and campaigns are discoverable without clutter.
*   **Search / Filter** — Search suggests while typing (product, brand, model) and remembers recent terms; listing pages let customers filter and sort with visible chips, clear counts and a predictable pagination model.
*   **Explore Products** — Listings show scannable product cards with readable price, savings and installment language; promotional and bundle cues appear inline where relevant.
*   **Compare / Decide** — Customers can add to compare in one tap from any listing, view an auto-populated side-by-side comparison with key differences surfaced, and see a total-cost row (product + delivery + install + energy → monthly EMI) alongside a humanized spec and warranty legend and inspectable imagery — so a complex TV, fridge or AC can be judged confidently without external search.
*   **Add to Cart** — Every add is confirmed with a mini-cart, bundles are grouped with saving visible, and the empty cart always offers a path forward.
*   **Checkout** — A visible three-step flow (`Customer & Delivery → Delivery & Payment → Review & Confirmation`) shows the full landed cost at every step, uses forgiving forms, handles errors inline, and keeps progress and trust cues visible on mobile.
*   **Order Confirmation** — A clear confirmation with order summary and a post-purchase status that can be tracked from the account area, carrying delivery and installation context forward.

**How friction is reduced:** At every transition — browse to listing, listing to product, product to cart, cart to checkout — the revamp shows *what changed, what is selected, and what happens next*, replaces recall (re-typing names) with recognition (chips, rails, compare bar), and reveals landed cost at the moment of price exposure rather than after commitment.

## 6. Optional Add-On: Personalized Product Recommendations

> **OPTIONAL ADD-ON SCOPE** — Clearly separated from the core UI/UX revamp above. The core revamp delivers value independently. Personalized recommendations can be considered separately based on business priorities and technical readiness.

The current site shows **no personalization in operation today** — only generic "Best Deals" style shelves. The opportunities below are therefore proposed as a **phased, optional layer** on top of the modernized core, not a prerequisite for it.

### What is included (validated opportunities)

| Capability | Description | Customer Value |
|---|---|---|
| **"You May Also Like"** | Related and alternative product suggestions on the product page | Easier discovery of comparable or complementary options without manual search |
| **Browsing-Behavior Recommendations** | Suggestions shaped by what a shopper has recently viewed or filtered | A homepage and listing that feels relevant instead of generic |
| **Previous-Purchase Suggestions** | For signed-in customers, suggestions informed by past orders (e.g., consumables, replacements, upgrades) | Faster path back to repeat needs |
| **Personalized Product Discovery** | Discovery surfaces weighted by individual browsing affinity | Less time spent scanning irrelevant categories |
| **Cross-Selling Recommendations** | Contextual suggestions at cart and checkout (accessories, complementary products) | Convenience of discovering relevant add-ons at the right moment |
| **Upselling Recommendations** | Contextual suggestions of a higher-tier model where relevant and genuinely comparable | Informed consideration of a tier above, without pressure |
| **Personalized Homepage** | Homepage modules reordered or weighted by individual shopping behavior on return visits | A more relevant first impression when returning |

### How to think about maturity

Recommendations should be approached in levels — not everything requires advanced AI from day one.

**Level 1 — Rule-Based Recommendations (can start early, low data dependency)**

Examples: Related products, complementary products, alternative products, frequently purchased together, manually curated cross-selling (e.g., *TV + wall mount + soundbar* as a curated bundle). These use product metadata, categories and curated pairings — *not* personal behavior — and can ship alongside the core revamp. This level already powers the `Complete the Setup` bundles in the core scope.

**Level 2 — Behavioral Personalization (needs lightweight behavioral signals)**

Examples: Recently viewed products, continue shopping (last filtered listing or search restored), browsing-behavior recommendations, category affinity. These use guest-session signals stored locally and can persist after sign-in. They are the most immediate personalization win — high value with the least infrastructure (the `Recently Viewed / Continue` memory already proposed in the core scope is the foundation for this level).

**Level 3 — Advanced AI-Driven Personalization (needs mature data)**

Examples: Personalized homepage reordering for returners, previous-purchase recommendations, intelligent cross-selling and upselling that adapts per individual, advanced affinity scoring and `Because you viewed` ranking. These require structured product attributes, reliable user behavior tracking, purchase history, analytics maturity and recommendation infrastructure.

### Phased approach

The current site's data and infrastructure have not been observed to support Level 3 today. The recommended path is:

1.  **Lightweight first step (near-term, alongside core):** Session-based `Recently Viewed` and rule-based related products — shippable without a personalization platform.
2.  **True personalization (optional, separately scoped):** Behavior- and history-based recommendations — scoped only once data collection, retention, product metadata quality, analytics pipeline and recommendation infrastructure are confirmed.

## 7. Scope Separation & Prioritization

### Core Priority — UI/UX Modernization (Primary)

The modernization and improvement of the **existing** e-commerce experience as described in Section 4 — including visual design, navigation, information architecture, product discovery, browsing, filtering, bundle and offer discovery, product experience, cart, the simple three-step checkout, responsive experience, usability, consistency and resolution of existing UX pain points. **This is the project.** It solves validated friction and delivers a visibly better shopping experience without depending on the add-on below.

### Optional Enhancement — Personalized Recommendations

Personalized and AI-driven product recommendation capabilities as described in Section 6 — browsing-behavior, previous-purchase, related/alternative, "You May Also Like", personalized homepage and discovery, cross-selling and upselling — organized by **Rule-Based → Behavioral → Advanced AI** maturity and proposed as a **separately estimated, optional** enhancement.

**The priority hierarchy is unmistakably clear:** Deliver the core UI/UX revamp first. Consider the add-on only when the core is stable and its data and infrastructure prerequisites are confirmed. The core must provide independent value; the add-on must not redefine or delay the primary objective.

## 9. Recommended Implementation Priorities

Organized as priorities, not timelines or effort estimates.

### Priority 1 — Core UX Foundations and Critical Friction Removal

Fix what blocks the journey and establish the trust row that every high-consideration purchase needs.

*   Repair broken browse paths (`See All` and category entry) and introduce link health monitoring
*   Restore working listing controls: active-filter chips with count and one-tap clear, real sort select, clear pagination, suppression of empty review filtering
*   Normalize warranty phrasing and deliver a plain-language warranty legend
*   Introduce delivery and serviceability estimation by area/district text input (not location permission) — the single source that also powers total-cost and store pickup
*   Show a transparent total-cost row on product pages (`Upfront + Delivery + Install + Energy → monthly EMI`) with tenure choices and an honest `ineligible → see EMI Bank List` verdict
*   Show plural payment choices equally (Cash on Delivery, bKash/Nagad, cards, EMI) with district-aware `COD available` truth
*   Provide authorized-retailer proof at the price context (not only on the homepage)
*   Deliver browse memory for guests: recently viewed rail and continue-shopping deep-link that restores filters
*   Ship intelligent search stage 1 (as-you-type suggestions by product/brand/category/SKU-exact plus recent searches)
*   Ship cart drawer and checkout shell for the three-step flow with mini-cart confirmation, landed-cost breakdown and toasts

*These are must-address — critical friction, journey blockers and serious trust issues for high-value purchases.*

### Priority 2 — Product Discovery, Browsing and Shopping Journey Improvements

Build on foundations to make research and shortlisting substantially easier.

*   Canonical and hygiene information-architecture fixes, re-layering of SEO category guides below product grids with a clear guide entry point
*   Persistent compare workspace (sticky bar → auto-populated `/compare` with key differences surfaced)
*   Guided selling finder for the highest-ticket family first (e.g., *What size AC do I need?* from room dimensions → tonnage), followed by refrigerator and TV lenses
*   Plain-language spec jargon decoder (`tap EER → estimated Tk/month`) for high-frequency terms
*   Rich-media P0 slice (hover zoom/pinch with gallery count) followed by short functional video per category
*   Variant and family navigator (sibling chips with price delta and stock status)
*   Installation feasibility checker (per-product checklist) plus priced `Add Installation` basket SKU
*   Human support spine refinement (sticky hotline, WhatsApp `Share via WhatsApp — Ask agent` deep-link, schedule-visit linkage)
*   Guest-to-signed-in continuity for saved browsing (migrating recently viewed and compare on sign-in)
*   Zero-result recovery (`Did you mean…`, facet relaxation with count preview, related suggestions + finder CTA)

*Strongly recommended — high user and business value once Priority 1 is stable.*

### Priority 3 — Advanced Experience Enhancements

Meaningful improvements with higher content or operational dependencies.

*   Homepage curation beyond budget — curated prioritization beyond simple grid subtraction
*   Curated `Complete the Setup` bundles piloted for high-consideration categories (with suppression for low-ticket items) before broader rollout
*   Open-box delivery eligibility badge by area, with full doorstep OTP and photo protocol phased after fleet confirmation
*   14-day hassle-free return badge and cross-category exchange value estimation, progressing toward AI-assisted diagnostics after demand is proven
*   Full ratings pipeline (collection, moderation, aggregation) promoted from the initial verified-review header badges
*   Additional guided-selling lenses (washing machine, then TV) once the AC lens proves the pattern and rule tables are governed
*   Rich media beyond zoom — dimension overlays from structured specs, and 360°/AR where assets exist

*Important but not immediately critical — gated by supply, content or operational proof.*

### Optional Add-On — Personalized and AI-Driven Recommendations

As described in Section 6, proposed and estimated separately in levels:

*   **Level 1 Rule-Based** — Related, alternative, complementary, frequently purchased together, curated cross-selling (can accompany Priority 1)
*   **Level 2 Behavioral** — Recently viewed, browsing-behavior, category affinity, continue shopping (needs lightweight behavioral signals already established by Priority 1 memory)
*   **Level 3 Advanced AI-Driven** — Previous-purchase recommendations, intelligent recommendations, personalized homepage reordering, advanced cross/upselling (requires confirmed data pipeline, purchase history, analytics maturity and recommendation infrastructure)

*Clearly separated scope. Level 1 can accompany the core; Levels 2–3 should follow only when data and infrastructure are ready.*

## 10. Scope Considerations

Relevant dependencies and considerations for planning and estimation:

*   The scope assumes a **UI/UX redesign and front-end rebuild**, not a backend platform migration. Existing payment and logistics integrations (COD, bKash, Nagad, cards, EMI) are assumed to remain — only their presentation is redesigned unless stated otherwise (e.g., the transparent total-cost row and plural payment truth row).
*   Product catalog data (images, specifications, categories and attributes) will need a cleanup and standardization pass for the new templates to display consistently — particularly warranty phrasing, specification keys and product family relationships.
*   Baseline behavioral and analytics tracking has not been observed on the current site; if none exists, instrumentation for `viewed product, filtered listing, searched term, compared, added to cart` will need to be scoped before or alongside any personalization.
*   Bundle and complementary logic is assumed to be **curated, manually-defined pairings at launch** (e.g., TV + mount, AC + stabilizer) rather than an automated bundling engine.
*   Personalized recommendations — especially Levels 2 and 3 — depend on **data readiness** (what browsing and order history is collected and retained), **product data quality** (structured, comparable attributes), **behavioral tracking**, **purchase history**, **analytics maturity**, and **recommendation infrastructure**. Effort varies significantly between rule-based and true AI-driven approaches; these should be confirmed directly with Transcom before final estimation of the add-on.
*   Any AI-driven capabilities beyond recommendations carry their own content and data prerequisites (e.g., AR-ready assets for room visualization, per-category feasibility rules for installation checking) and warrant their own discovery pass if the client wishes to pursue them.

This is not a technical architecture document — dependencies are stated at the planning level so estimates can be framed accurately.

## 11. Conclusion & Recommended Next Steps

**Where the platform is today:** A functionally complete catalog with credible breadth — but an interaction-immature experience where the path from discovery to decision carries friction at exactly the points where appliance buyers need confidence.

**Biggest opportunity:** The first priority is to **modernize and simplify the existing shopping experience** — cleaner visual design, reliable navigation, working browse controls, and a landed-cost-transparent path from product to three-step checkout. This alone removes the friction that currently costs the most journeys. The single highest-leverage investment is the **delivery and total-cost truth row** (area text → estimate + EMI + plural payment), because it fixes price surprise for every high-value purchase.

**What should happen first:** Deliver the **core UI/UX revamp (Priority 1 foundations)** from discovery through checkout as described in Sections 4.1–4.12. This establishes the trust row, memory, and browse hygiene that every later enhancement depends on.

**Vision:** A need-led electronics retailer where customers are helped to choose the right specification, shown what it will really cost to own monthly, and supported by human reassurance where needed — not a catalog that requires external search to decode.

**Recommended next steps:**

| Role | Action |
|---|---|
| **Business stakeholders** | Approve the core UI/UX revamp as the primary commissioned scope.
